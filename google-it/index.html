Just Fucking Google It

"Hey, quick question..."

No. Fuck off.

That's not a "quick question." That's you being a leech. Something you could find in 10 seconds on Google, but you just had to open your mouth instead. Ping someone on Slack, tap them on the shoulder, @mention in the group chat — you got your answer nice and easy. The person you interrupted? Train of thought gone. Code they were writing, wasted. You traded 30 minutes of someone else's focus for 10 seconds of your own laziness. You think that's a fair deal?

"What's Wrong With Just Asking?" — Everything

You ask, they stop. That stop costs 23 minutes of deep focus — that's actual research, not something I made up. Your "how do I convert a string to int in Python" question — is it worth 23 minutes of someone's life? You know damn well that shit takes 3 seconds to google. You just couldn't be arsed.

You say "it's faster to just ask." Yeah, faster for YOU. You flap your lips, someone else burns brain cells for you. You're like the person at a restaurant asking the waiter "what's in this dish?" while holding the fucking menu. Difference is, the waiter gets paid. Your coworker doesn't get a cent from you. So why the hell should they serve you?

"But they already know the answer!" So does Google. Google also won't talk shit about you behind your back. Google is online 7*24h, no waiting for a reply, no mood swings. What happens when your coworker is on vacation? You just shut down?

The Freeloader's Price List

"It's broken" — Two words. Says absolutely nothing. Screenshot? Logs? What's broken? Which file? Which line? What did you do to trigger it? Nothing. Just two words lobbed into the chat, expecting someone to read your goddamn mind. A question like this should cost you real money before anyone even looks at it.

"It won't run, what do I do?" — Slightly better. At least you described a symptom. But it's still useless. What won't run? Frontend? Backend? Local? Production? What OS? What version? Did you try anything? Did you read the error? Barely an upgrade, still a losing proposition for whoever has to deal with you.

"I ran XX command, got this error (screenshot attached), docs say to do XX but it still fails" — Now you're talking like a human being. You searched. You tried. You described the problem clearly. People will actually help you for free. Because you proved you're not just leeching off someone's time — you're genuinely stuck.

What's the difference? Whether you used your brain first.

Exhibit A

Any engineering Slack channel, any day of the week:

Dave: hey how do I convert a string to int in Python?
Sarah: int()
Dave: thanks! how do I read a file?
Sarah: open()
Dave: cool. what's the difference between a list and a tuple?
Sarah: (typing indicator appears and disappears three times)
Sarah: google it
Dave: wow ok rude

Dave asked three questions in ten minutes. Every single one is the first result on Google. The first result! Not buried on page five of some dead forum. The literal first blue link. Sarah was debugging a production incident. She stopped three times to be Dave's personal search engine. Dave thinks Sarah is rude.

Every team has a Dave. Think about whether there's one around you. Then think about whether it's you.

You Think You're Saving Time. You're Practicing Being Useless

When you find something yourself, you remember it, because you went through the process. You skimmed a few posts, ruled out wrong answers, compared different takes, and finally got it. That process IS the learning.

When someone chews the food and spits it into your mouth, you swallow without even chewing. Tomorrow you're hungry again. Day after that you come back asking the exact same question. Doesn't that embarrass you even a little?

Every team has that person — been there three years and still asks how to resolve merge conflicts, how to write regex, how to set environment variables. Everyone knows what's going on. Nobody wants to say it to their face. Silence doesn't mean nobody noticed. People just figure it's pointless because this person will ask again next time anyway. You want to be that person? Then keep freeloading.

You, Five Years From Now

Fast forward five years. You're a "senior engineer" now. Got the title, got the raise. But you can't debug on your own. Every unfamiliar error sends you into a panic. Every new framework is a deer-in-headlights moment. You don't speak up during tech discussions, because you've never actually researched anything yourself — for five years, other people did the searching for you.

You're not a senior engineer. You're a junior who sat in a chair for five years. You know it. Your coworkers know it. Your boss knows it. Nobody says it out loud because telling someone "you've been here five years and you still can't do shit" is too fucking awkward. Everyone would rather keep answering your dumb questions than have that conversation.

Languages go obsolete. Frameworks get replaced. But "look it up yourself before asking" — that skill never expires. If you won't even practice that, what the hell are you practicing?

Stop Freeloading. Do This Instead

Before you open your mouth:

  • Search it. Spend at least 5 minutes actually reading results, not just headlines.
  • Read the docs. Your tools have documentation. Read it.
  • Search your codebase. A quick grep won't kill you.
  • Try it yourself. Got an error? Read the error message. It's written for humans, not pigs.
  • Search your chat history. Someone almost certainly already answered this.

Tried all five and still stuck? Fine, ask. But tell them what you did, what you expected, what actually happened, what you already looked up, what you already tried. That's called asking a question. What you were doing before? That's called begging.

Good questions get people racing to answer. Bad questions get you muted.

Google is right there.
The docs are right there.
Stack Overflow, GitHub, Reddit — all right there.
Your brain is also right there (well, maybe not).
Stop pretending they don't exist.

Just fucking google it.
Or at the very least, ask an AI. Even the dumbest one.
