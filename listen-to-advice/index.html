Just Fucking Listen to Advice

"I need help."

Cool. Then shut the hell up for ten seconds and listen.

Asking for help is not the problem. Ignoring every answer is. You ask the same question three times, reject every solution, repeat the exact same mistake, then act surprised when things burn again. That's not bad luck. That's you smearing your own mess onto everyone else.

The Performance of Pretending to Learn

You don't actually want advice. You want emotional support for the decision you've already made. You want people to bless your bad plan so you can fail with confidence.

"Should I install Steam on the production server?" No. "Use steamcmd and keep the machine clean." Got it. Next week: Steam is there. A game booster is there. Some random unzip tool is there. Then you ask again like this is an unsolved philosophical debate.

It's not a debate. It's instructions. You asked for them. Either follow them or stop jerking everyone around.

Reality Check: Your Refusal Has a Bill

Every ignored warning becomes someone else's emergency. The person helping you now has to clean your machine, trace your weird changes, and explain the same basics like you're allergic to memory and proud of it.

And while they're fixing the mess you recreated on purpose, actual work stops. Releases slip. Incidents multiply. Team trust dies one "next time for sure" at a time.

Concrete Example: "Just Looking" in Production

You were told: "Don't log into the server now. I'm deploying." What do you do? You log in anyway because you're "just looking."

Your session steals focus, collides with the active operation, and now two people are fighting the same box at the same time. Congratulations. You turned a controlled change into chaos because curiosity mattered more to you than coordination.

"I was only checking" is not an excuse. It's a confession that you were meddling.

Future You Is Not Special

Keep doing this and your reputation writes itself: asks for help, ignores help, repeats failure. People will stop explaining things to you because explanation clearly doesn't survive contact with your ego.

Then one day you'll ask a real urgent question, and the room will go quiet. Not because nobody knows. Because nobody believes you'll listen for even five damn seconds.

The Rule

If you ask for advice, treat it like a contract. Ask clearly. Confirm what you heard. Execute it. If you disagree, say it before you act—not after the damage.

Stop saying "next time" while recreating the same disaster.

Either listen and execute, or shut up and stop asking.

Direct. Rude. Unapologetic.
Because sugar-coating bad habits never fixed anything.
